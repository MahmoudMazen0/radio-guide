Where the milestones sit in the radio

Every amplitude-modulation receiver has these two halves at its front: a tuned circuit picks one station, and a detector takes the sound back out of its carrier. The rest of the term adds the gain around them.

One station, one envelope

The tank passes a single station’s amplitude-modulated wave; the detector keeps its envelope — the sound. A crystal radio is nothing more than these two parts and a sensitive earphone, powered by the station itself. The 1930s-style radio we build adds gain before the detector, so weak stations rise above the diode’s dead zone, and after it, so the sound can drive a speaker.

The milestones

M1 · Tuner & detector

A coil, a tuning capacitor and a peak rectifier

Wheeler’s formula, a hand-wound 75-turn coil, and a tank that resonated at 620 kHz instead of the predicted 819 — plus the stray capacitance that explains the gap. Then Q, and an envelope detector that turns an AM wave into a DC level and a sound. Read it at M1.

Two CE stages before the detector

Coming after LAB3: a link coil, two common-emitter stages and a 1 kΩ potentiometer — the station M1’s diode cannot yet hear.
